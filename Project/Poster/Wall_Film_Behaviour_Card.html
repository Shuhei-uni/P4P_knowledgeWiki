05 · WALL FILM

Wall-film behaviour

6.69 → 9.54 kgearly-run film mass · 265 → 500 ms

Wall liquid can spread, drain or return to the steam as droplets. Different runs reveal these different parts of its behaviour.

1 · Film spreads and stores liquid

Early runs showed a broader, thicker film over the lower and middle walls. The upper wall remained thinner.

Same camera and 0–0.30 mm scale. These early snapshots have release models OFF; the rate history continues to 500 ms.

2 · Added models represent liquid returning to the flow

Stripping: steam shear removes film droplets. The literature sketch gives the physical rationale.

Splashing: droplet impacts create more droplets. Added as a separate impact model.

Edge separation: film leaves wall edges as droplets. Added as a separate edge model.

Later runs enabled these processes and droplet collection. They add liquid-transfer paths; they do not resolve every breakup event.

3 · Rates distinguish drainage, release and accumulation

Later run · release plus film absorber

Mean rates in a 75 ms window before the numerical guard.

Stripping and edge separation transfer liquid to the flow. The film absorber removes mass and momentum to represent entry into the brine pool. No isolated splash rate was recorded.

What we learnt: active lower-film removal can coexist with continued upper-film accumulation. Film growth, drainage and droplet release must be assessed together.

Two different model-development runs; bulk flow was frozen during these film windows. Steady film was not reached. The later run stopped at a numerical guard and retains source-accounting and inner-solve limits; its transfer rates do not establish separator efficiency.
