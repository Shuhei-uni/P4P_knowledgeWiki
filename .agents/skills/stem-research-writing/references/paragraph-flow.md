# Paragraph flow and emphasis

## Diagnose the connection

Give each paragraph a main job: establish a gap, describe a method, report a comparison, explain a mechanism, or qualify a conclusion. State that job in a short phrase before revising a difficult paragraph. Split or move material when it performs a different job.

Introduce the main point early enough for the reader to understand the supporting detail. Use familiar information to connect a sentence to the preceding discussion, then introduce the new point. Place important information where the sentence gives it appropriate emphasis, often near the end. Treat these as flexible principles rather than a template.

Keep the subject and verb close enough for the action to remain clear. Choose active or passive voice partly according to the topic the paragraph is following. Repeat a precise technical term when that repetition maintains the connection.

## Repair logic before adding transitions

Identify the intended relationship between neighbouring sentences: evidence, consequence, contrast, condition, example, or sequence. State the missing relationship when it can be supported. A transition word cannot supply missing evidence.

Check words such as "therefore", "however", and "similarly" against the actual relationship. Replace vague references such as "this" with the relevant noun when more than one interpretation is possible. Keep a transition brief when the relationship is already clear.

Read the paragraph's first and last sentences together. Check that the ending delivers or appropriately qualifies the opening. Avoid adding a redundant summary to every paragraph. Then read the topic sentences across the section to check the larger flow.

## Illustrative example

"Liquid entered the wall film throughout the run. The film inventory continued to increase during the sampled period. This increase indicates that the recorded inventory had not yet stabilised."

The example is invented. Its repeated subject connects a stated observation to a bounded interpretation. Do not reuse it as evidence about a real case.
