# Writing results

## Present what the evidence establishes

Organise the section around the relevant questions, comparisons, or findings. Briefly remind the reader why a comparison matters when its purpose is not obvious. Report the relevant observations, their magnitude and conditions, and the figure or table that supports them.

Use a flexible sequence: question or finding, supporting evidence, important qualification, connection to the next point. Do not force every paragraph into an identical template. Keep extended explanation of mechanisms and broader implications for the discussion unless the report combines results and discussion.

## Make comparisons complete

Name the variable, reference case, direction and size of the difference, and the conditions under which the comparison holds. Identify uncertainty or variation where available. Keep actual observations separate from the explanation proposed for them.

Do not turn every row of a table into a sentence. Select the features needed to evaluate the argument, while retaining access to the underlying results. Give exceptions or conflicting observations enough space to make their effect on the conclusion clear.

Keep pre-specified and exploratory analyses distinguishable. If some cases were omitted or a run failed, state this when it changes the comparison or scope. Do not present an incomplete subset as the full experiment.

## Use direct language

Illustrative only: "In the supplied test, the mean pressure drop was 12 kPa for Case A and 15 kPa for Case B over the same sampling interval. Case A therefore had a 20% lower pressure drop relative to Case B. The comparison does not identify the cause of the difference."

Use numbers only when supported by the actual inputs. If a figure supports only a qualitative observation, label it accordingly. Do not infer exact values, significance, or a mechanism from a visual impression.

Check that every stated result has a method, every figure reference points to the intended evidence, and every broader conclusion is developed where interpretation belongs.

Basis: [MIT Communication Lab, Results](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/be/commkit/journal-article-results/). The reporting checks and example are original applications.
