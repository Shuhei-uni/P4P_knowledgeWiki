# Words and the author's voice

## Make language clear and precise

Preserve the author's recognisable voice and correct existing wording when it already works. Use accessible academic English without making the science vague. Retain necessary technical vocabulary, symbols, and established terminology. Follow the user's requested English variety; otherwise preserve the document's convention, with New Zealand English as the personal default for a new report.

Prefer a familiar word when it has the same meaning. Remove ornamental formality, vague praise, and unnecessary em dashes. Do not replace every repeated word with a synonym: a new technical label may imply a new concept.

| Wording to inspect | Possible edit | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| utilise | use | Same meaning in context |
| in order to | to | No needed distinction is lost |
| a comparison was undertaken | we compared, or the cases were compared | Preserve the appropriate actor and focus |
| better performance | Name the improved metric | Evidence identifies the metric |
| improved realism | Describe added physics or measured agreement | Match the evidence actually available |
| novel method | Describe the specific contribution | Novelty has not been established |

Treat the table as prompts for judgement, not global replacements.

## Use voice for a reason

Use active voice when the actor matters or its omission creates ambiguity. Use passive voice when the procedure, object, or continuing topic deserves focus. Follow the document's convention for "I" or "we". First person and passive voice can both be appropriate in scientific writing.

Keep uncertainty when it expresses a real limitation. Replace vague claims such as "significant improvement" with the measured change unless the intended kind of significance is established. Use accurate contribution verbs, including "modified" and "combined", when those describe the work.

Illustrative only: "The setting substantially improved the simulation" could become "The setting reduced the mean mass imbalance" if that is what the evidence shows. The revision narrows the statement to a particular observation; do not make that change silently during a grammar-only edit.

Basis: [MIT, Voice](https://web.mit.edu/21.guide/voice.htm) and [MIT, Accuracy](https://web.mit.edu/21.guide/accuracy.htm). Voice preservation and language defaults are personal design choices.
