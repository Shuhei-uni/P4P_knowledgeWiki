# Revision and review

## Match the intervention to the request

| Request | Main work | Preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plan a report | Questions, supported findings, section sequence | Brief, scope, unresolved work |
| Draft a section | Complete prose from available evidence | Findings, conditions, source attribution |
| Improve flow | Order and connections at the requested scale | Scientific meaning and required structure |
| Edit grammar | Local corrections and clear phrasing | Argument, claim strength, numbers, terminology |
| Shorten | Remove repetition and peripheral explanation | Necessary evidence, limitations, reproducibility details |
| Expand | Explain reasoning or supplied evidence more fully | Boundary between known and missing information |
| Critique | Prioritise concrete problems and repairs | The author's intent and valid existing work |

For a substantive revision, address argument and evidence before paragraph order, sentence clarity, and word choice. For a narrow language edit, stay within its scope and flag a material scientific issue separately.

## Review with concrete questions

Check whether the report answers its stated objectives; whether its important findings are visible; and whether the conclusion is supported by the methods and results. Check contradictions, missing comparisons, causal overreach, and the effect of limitations. Then check paragraph purpose, transitions, sentence meaning, grammar, and consistent terminology.

If a claim changes, inspect its repeated appearances in the abstract, results, discussion, conclusion, captions, and tables. A smoother paragraph is not a successful edit if it contradicts another section.

Give actionable feedback: identify the location or quoted phrase, explain its consequence, and propose a specific repair. Distinguish a factual or logical problem from an optional style preference. Prioritise the few changes that most improve correctness or comprehension; avoid a mechanical catalogue of minor edits.

## Deliver useful work

Ask early about unresolved choices in the argument, emphasis, scope, or depth of revision. Pair each question with a suggested direction and reason, using the user's existing answers wherever available. Before changing the central message or removing a major finding, discuss the choice when the request has not already settled it.

Return the requested draft, revision, or critique. Add concise notes on substantive changes or unresolved evidence where useful or requested. Keep unanswered editorial choices provisional and missing scientific facts unresolved. Complete independent work while awaiting input; routine grammar edits do not need a review checkpoint.

Stop when the requested scope is complete and the relevant checks are satisfied. Do not repeatedly rewrite sound prose to make changes visible. Do not claim that a language or consistency review certifies the underlying research.

Basis for placing language editing after structural revision: [MIT, Editing for grammar and style](https://web.mit.edu/21.guide/editing.htm). The task routing and review procedure are original operational guidance.
