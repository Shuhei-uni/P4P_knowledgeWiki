# Writing about figures, tables, and equations

## Give each display a clear job

Identify the question a figure, table, or equation helps answer. Connect it to the relevant claim in the prose. Describe the evidence the reader should notice and why it matters. Avoid a paragraph that says only that a figure exists, or that narrates every plotted point.

Inspect the actual display and its source data when available. Check labels, units, conditions, normalisation, time intervals, and uncertainty against the prose. Distinguish a conceptual illustration from measured or simulated evidence. Do not use an illustrative diagram as proof of the proposed mechanism.

## Write useful captions

Include the applicable information:

- What is shown and what each panel represents.
- Conditions and comparisons needed to interpret the display.
- Meanings of symbols, lines, colours, and abbreviations that are not otherwise clear.
- Units, sample counts, aggregation, and uncertainty definitions where relevant.
- The source or adaptation credit if required.

Use a takeaway in the caption when it is supported and fits the venue. Keep speculative interpretation in the discussion. Make a caption sufficiently self-contained without repeating the full methods section.

## Explain equations as part of the argument

Introduce why the equation is needed. Define unfamiliar symbols, units or dimensions, assumptions, and the domain where it applies. Explain what the equation enables the reader to calculate or understand. Distinguish an established equation, a derived result, and an empirical fit.

Preserve mathematical signs, subscripts, superscripts, indices, labels, and cross-references during prose editing. Flag a suspected mathematical inconsistency rather than silently changing the science. Use the applicable document tools when equation rendering or layout needs work.

Illustrative only: replace "Figure 5 shows the results" with a sentence naming the supported comparison, such as "Figure 5 compares the outlet liquid flux across the three cases." Add a conclusion only after inspecting the evidence.

These are original writing checks. For source background, see [Sources](sources.md). Visual design, colour selection, plotting, and document layout are separate operations.
