# Title, abstract, and conclusion

## Keep the beginning and ending aligned

Draft these elements from the supported main text. Check them again after substantive changes. Preserve the same scope, definitions, and principal numbers across the report. Do not compress away a qualification that changes the meaning of the central claim.

| Element | Job | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | Identify the topic and meaningful contribution or question | Does its wording claim more than the study establishes? |
| Abstract | Give a standalone account of the problem, approach, key findings, and meaning | Can a reader understand what was learned without opening the report? |
| Conclusion | Answer the original objectives using the completed work | Is each answer supported earlier, with its important limits retained? |

## Write the abstract as a complete account

Follow the required format and word limit. Where none is supplied, use a concise paragraph appropriate to the report rather than assuming a universal length. Include only the method details needed to understand the result. Select representative quantitative findings when available; explain enough about the comparator and conditions to make them meaningful.

Use a result-focused abstract for completed research. Use proposal language for planned work. Avoid an abstract that only lists what each chapter discusses. Check whether the venue permits citations before adding them.

## Make the conclusion answer the question

Return to each principal objective, state the answer or partial answer, and explain its practical or scientific implication within the tested scope. Include the most consequential unresolved issue when it affects the answer. Keep detailed mechanisms and literature comparisons in the discussion.

Do not introduce new results. Avoid repeating the entire abstract or methods. Include recommendations only when requested or appropriate to the report, and link each recommendation to evidence and any conditions for its use.

Illustrative title distinction: "Assessing wall-film behaviour in a separator model" identifies an investigation. "An optimised and validated separator model" additionally claims completed optimisation and validation, which require evidence.

Basis: [Mensh and Kording, Ten simple rules for structuring papers](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005619). The report-specific checks and example are original applications.
