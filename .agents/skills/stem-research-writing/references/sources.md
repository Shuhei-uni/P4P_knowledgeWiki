# Sources and scope of the guidance

Reviewed on 7 October 2026, New Zealand time. Use these links to inspect the basis of the writing guidance. They do not provide evidence for the user's project findings. Re-check current venue requirements and technical software documentation when a task depends on them.

The skill's instructions, tables, and examples are an original synthesis. Public agent skills were compared for useful design patterns; their files were not copied. Examples labelled illustrative or invented are not project data. Editorial guidance is adaptable to the user's brief and discipline.

## Primary writing and research guidance

- Mensh, B., and Kording, K. (2017). [Ten simple rules for structuring papers](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005619). PLOS Computational Biology, 13(9), e1005619. Supports argument-led organisation and alignment of the report's main parts. Adapt journal-article advice to project reports and theses.
- MIT Nuclear Science and Engineering Communication Lab. [Journal article: Introduction](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/nse/commkit/journal-article-introduction/). Supports relevant background, a supported gap, and a clear aim.
- MIT EECS Communication Lab. [Paper: Methods (EE)](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/eecs/commkit/journal-article-methods-ee/). Supports reproducibility, methodological justification, and useful detail.
- MIT Biological Engineering Communication Lab. [Journal article: Results](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/be/commkit/journal-article-results/). Supports evidence organised around findings. Its discipline-specific presentation suggestions are not universal rules.
- MIT Biological Engineering Communication Lab. [Journal article: Discussion](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/be/commkit/journal-article-discussion/). Supports interpretation, comparison with prior work, and consequential limitations.
- Gopen, G. D., and Swan, J. A. (1990). [The science of scientific writing](https://www.cs.tufts.edu/comp/150FP/archive/george-gopen/sci.html). American Scientist, 78(6), 550-558; authorised reproduction hosted at Tufts. Supports reader-oriented sentence organisation. Apply its principles flexibly.
- Duke Graduate School. [Cohesion, coherence, and emphasis](https://sites.duke.edu/scientificwriting/lesson-2-cohesion-coherence-and-emphasis/). Supports paragraph continuity and purposeful voice choices.
- MIT technical writing guide. [Editing for grammar and style](https://web.mit.edu/21.guide/editing.htm), [Sequence of tenses](https://web.mit.edu/21.guide/sequence.htm), [Common ESL writing problems](https://web.mit.edu/21.guide/esl-link.htm), [Voice](https://web.mit.edu/21.guide/voice.htm), and [Accuracy](https://web.mit.edu/21.guide/accuracy.htm). Supports local language diagnostics and technical precision.
- University of Manchester. [Academic Phrasebank: Being cautious](https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/using-cautious-language/). Supports calibrated explanations and limited generalisation. Use the rhetorical functions rather than reproducing stock phrases.
- American Statistical Association (2016). [Official statement release on statistical significance and p-values](https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/p-valuestatement.pdf). Supports the distinction between statistical significance, effect size, and substantive importance.
- NASA NPARC Alliance CFD tutorial. [Verification assessment](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/verassess.html), [Validation assessment](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/valassess.html), and [Iterative convergence](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/wind/valid/tutorial/iterconv.html). Supports distinctions used in simulation reporting. These established tutorial pages are not presented as the latest formal standard or a source of universal thresholds.
- Agent Skills. [Specification](https://agentskills.io/specification). Supports one SKILL.md entry point with focused resources loaded as needed.

## Apply the guidance responsibly

Treat the brief, supplied evidence, and relevant disciplinary requirements as the basis of the actual report. Use a writing source to guide communication, not to establish the truth of a technical result. Check a source's exact proposition before citing it in a report. Keep copied quotations brief and attributed; consult licences before reusing source text or assets.
