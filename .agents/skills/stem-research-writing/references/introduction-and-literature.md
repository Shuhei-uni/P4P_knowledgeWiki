# Introduction and literature synthesis

## Establish a precise reason for the study

Give the intended reader enough background to understand the problem, the relevant prior work, the remaining question, and the study's aim. Narrow the introduction toward the work actually presented. Avoid a broad history of the field unless that history is necessary to the argument.

Make the gap specific and supported. A gap can be an untested condition, a conflicting result, a limitation of a method, or an unresolved practical question. Do not invent a universal absence of research because a small search found few papers. Separate a literature gap from a limitation of the current project.

State what the study evaluates or develops, its scope, and the questions it can answer. Use verbs that accurately describe the contribution: reproduce, compare, assess, modify, extend, derive, or develop. Reserve novelty claims for a supported comparison with prior work.

## Synthesize the literature

Organise prior studies around the question they help answer. For a substantial review, compare them using relevant dimensions such as assumptions, methods, operating conditions, measures, conclusions, and limitations. Explain agreements and disagreements; identify whether differences may arise from different conditions or definitions.

Use individual-paper summaries when one study is a direct baseline or when its details are essential. Otherwise, connect studies into a reasoned account instead of writing an annotated bibliography in paragraph form. Describe earlier research fairly, including strengths that the current work retains.

## Preserve the introduction's promises

Check that every stated objective has a corresponding method and result, or is explicitly identified as outside the completed scope. If an objective remains unfinished, explain that status instead of rewriting history to imply it was never intended. Keep proposed future optimisation separate from achieved improvements.

Illustrative outline: explain a device's relevant function; identify what a prior model represented; identify the specific unresolved behaviour; state how the present study investigates that behaviour. Populate this outline only with verified project details and sources.

Basis: [MIT Nuclear Science and Engineering Communication Lab, Introduction](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/nse/commkit/journal-article-introduction/). The literature-comparison and project-scope checks are original applications.
