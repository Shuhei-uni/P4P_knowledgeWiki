# Claims and uncertainty

## Match the statement to its evidence

Distinguish what was measured, calculated, assumed, inferred, or proposed. State a recorded result directly; qualify the uncertain interpretation. Attach the qualification to the part of the sentence that needs it.

| Claim type | Useful wording | Required care |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct observation | increased, was measured, was observed | Name the quantity and conditions |
| Model output | the model predicted, the calculation yielded | Keep prediction distinct from physical observation |
| Supported interpretation | suggests, supports, is consistent with | Explain what evidence supports the interpretation |
| Possible mechanism | may arise from, could reflect | Identify unresolved alternatives |
| Assumption | assumes, treats as, was prescribed | Do not present it as a finding |
| Proposal | could be tested, is proposed | Keep expected benefits conditional |

These expressions are not a universal confidence scale. Choose the wording that represents the relationship, and explain consequential uncertainty explicitly. "Is consistent with" does not show that an explanation is unique.

## Inspect claims that often expand during editing

Check "proves", "causes", "best", "optimal", "robust", "accurate", "validated", "significant", and "realistic". Ask what evidence and scope each word requires. Retain the word when justified; otherwise narrow it, or flag it if the request permits language changes only.

Do not turn association into causation, a model assumption into an observed mechanism, or a result within tested cases into a universal conclusion. State the domain, conditions, and relevant uncertainty. Avoid adding several hedges when one precise qualification is clearer.

Illustrative only: "Film inventory increased during the sampled period. Continued liquid deposition may explain the increase, although the available results do not isolate the contribution of each film process." The first sentence reports an observation. The second offers a bounded explanation.

For incomplete evidence, omit the unsupported extension, mark a specific gap, or write a conditional interpretation. Do not fill the gap with a plausible reference or stronger wording.

Basis: [Manchester Academic Phrasebank, Being cautious](https://www.phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk/using-cautious-language/). The claim categories and scientific checks are original operational guidance.
