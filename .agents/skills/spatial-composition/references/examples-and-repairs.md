# Examples and repairs

These are constructed teaching examples, not screenshots of completed P4P work.
Use them to learn a relationship, not to copy a mandatory layout.

## Example 1: lively background, clear subject

**Problem:** a vivid field image is equally detailed everywhere. Its bright curves
cross the title and the separator outline. The first response is to fade the
entire background until its character disappears.

**Alternative:** retain expressive detail at the edges; move the crop and apply a
separate local mask behind the vessel and text. Keep the mask independent of the
scientific source. Let the subject cross a supporting region's boundary while
keeping charts and captions unobstructed.

```text
Back:      expressive field ~~~ [quieter silhouette region] ~~~ field
Middle:    supporting group          |          supporting group
Subject:                         SEPARATOR
Front:                     small label -> real target
```

**Readback:** the environment feels energetic; the separator remains recognisable;
the label is readable but not the first thing noticed. Failure means either the
background still wins or the repair has erased the desired atmosphere.

## Example 2: topmost does not mean most prominent

**Problem:** callouts have bright fills, thick outlines and large type because they
are in the foreground. They compete with the object they explain.

**Alternative:** keep callouts above the image but reduce container emphasis. Use
clear text, enough local contrast, a short leader, and spacing that associates each
callout with its target. The callout's draw order stays high; its attention rank
becomes supporting.

**Readback:** the viewer notices the object first and can inspect its details next.
The repair fails if the callouts become unreadable or their targets ambiguous.

## Example 3: connected composition instead of equal tiles

**Problem:** every figure, title and paragraph sits in an identical card. The
alignment is tidy, but the intended main object is just another tile.

**Alternative:** establish the main object's scale and clear space. Group related
support by proximity and shared alignment. Remove containers only where they do
not encode a useful grouping. Use one deliberate overlap or frame crossing when
it makes the subject's position apparent.

**Readback:** the main object and supporting clusters have different jobs. This
approach is unsuitable when equally weighted panels are needed for comparison.
A comparative chart grid is not a design failure merely because it uses a grid.

## Example 4: small detail with a purpose

**Problem:** a strong main illustration and a large title feel disconnected. The
agent adds glow, a decorative badge, and multiple divider lines.

**Alternative:** test a short framing line placed between the title and main field,
or align the title edge with a meaningful illustration boundary. Use approved copy
or propose new copy separately in visual-only work. Keep the added element small
but readable and give it one clear function.

**Readback:** the title and subject feel related. The detail disappears as a
competitor at thumbnail scale, but adds context on closer inspection.

## Example 5: the right answer can be less depth

**Problem:** a monitoring dashboard receives tilted charts, parallax, and large
illustrations because the prompt says to make it more dimensional.

**Alternative:** preserve scan order and comparisons. Use clear grouping and only
the elevation needed for active menus, dialogs or selected content. Reserve literal
perspective for a subject that needs it.

**Readback:** the task becomes easier, not merely more theatrical. Composition
serves the medium and audience rather than a universal dramatic style.

## Analyse a real reference

Use an actual image when the user provides one or reference research is needed.
Record its source and separate these four observations:

| Observation | Example |
|---|---|
| Visible mechanism | The subject overlaps a boundary; its silhouette has a calm surround. |
| Effect inferred | The subject appears separate from the background. |
| Transfer to this brief | Try the boundary crossing with our verified subject asset. |
| Do not transfer | The unrelated brand, artwork, copy, or data colours. |

Words such as premium, clean, cinematic, or professional are not explanations.
Point to the edge, spacing, crop or contrast that produces the claimed effect.
