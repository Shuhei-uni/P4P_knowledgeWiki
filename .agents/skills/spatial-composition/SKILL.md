# Spatial composition

Design relationships before decoration. Decide what recedes, what organises,
and what attracts attention, then verify those decisions in the rendered work.
Depth is a compositional tool, not a requirement to make every design look 3D.

## 1. Establish the visual brief

Read the selected artifact, its design definition, supplied references, and the
latest user instructions. Identify the audience, medium, intended viewing size,
requested deliverable, available assets, and what must remain unchanged.
New explicit instructions replace conflicting older preferences only on the
changed axes. Keep unchanged requirements intact.

For a visual-only task, preserve claims, values, captions, and evidence selection.
Route scientific figure production to `cfd-numerical-analysis`, argument/prose to
`stem-research-writing`, and report assembly to `report-writing` when needed.
For a P4P poster, read [the project adapter](references/p4p-poster.md).

Ask only a missing question that materially changes the composition, with a
recommended option. Reuse answers already given. Continue reversible exploration
under a stated assumption rather than making routine choices into approval gates.
**Done:** the visual goal, protected content, and unresolved choices are explicit.

## 2. Define the relationships

Use [principles](references/principles.md) when choosing depth cues or diagnosing
competition. Record three separate orders:

- **Depth:** what sits behind, overlaps, or shares a plane with what.
- **Attention:** what should be noticed first, next, and on close inspection.
- **Reading:** how the information becomes understandable.

For each major element, name its role, neighbours, allowed overlaps, protected
clear space, and the visible cue that should establish its place. A topmost label
can remain visually quiet. A large background can remain subordinate.
Reuse the artifact's design definition; [the compact template](templates/DESIGN.md)
is available when none exists. Keep project decisions with the artifact, not here.
**Done:** every major element has a job and a relationship, not just styling.

## 3. Explore composition, not cosmetic variants

For new directions, compare a small number of arrangements using the same content
and assets. Vary placement, scale, crop, overlap, or negative space. For a targeted
repair, keep the selected arrangement and skip unrelated alternatives.

Study references through observable mechanisms: describe where edges overlap,
where detail becomes quiet, and how groups connect. Use
[examples and repairs](references/examples-and-repairs.md) for calibration.
**Done:** each proposed variant tests a different spatial idea; name the strongest
candidate and its trade-off. If the user asked for concepts only, stop here.

## 4. Construct editable layers

Read [editable construction](references/editable-construction.md). Use the
requested platform and its available tools. Keep text, figures, backgrounds,
masks, and callouts independently editable; group elements by their relationships.
Image generation may supply individual assets, not a flattened substitute for
the whole composition. Separate atmospheric imagery from scientific evidence.
**Done:** the composition can be revised locally without regenerating everything.

## 5. Render, read back, repair

Read [render and critique](references/render-and-critique.md). Inspect the actual
render at thumbnail, whole-artifact, and close-reading scales. Describe the
observed hierarchy before comparing it with the intended hierarchy.

Repair the most important mismatch using the smallest meaningful change. Consider
quieting a competitor before strengthening the focal element. Re-render the
changed region and the whole composition; retain improvements and revert losses.
**Done:** the intended anchor and groups are apparent, text/evidence remain legible,
and overlap helps rather than hides. Without rendering access, label the result
an unverified composition proposal, not a visually validated artifact.

## 6. Deliver at the requested level

Return the requested concepts, critique, or editable artifact with its preview.
State the key relationships, what changed, and any remaining verification limits.
Do not replace a design request with a long method/results discussion.

For provenance or further research, read [sources](references/sources.md).
When changing this skill, use [evaluation scenarios](evals/scenarios.md); structural
checks alone do not establish better visual judgement.
