# Research and challenge

Use this branch when a proposed mechanism, bold probe, or consequential
inference needs stronger context before compute or before changing direction.

## Research in layers

Start with the exact scientific tension: the observation, mechanism, model
choice, or missing capability that could alter the next experiment. Then gather
only evidence capable of changing that decision:

1. Search the current `Project/` record for prior runs, rejected routes, and
   unresolved assumptions.
2. Use `cfd-wiki` for reusable method evidence and maintained literature
   pointers.
3. Use live Fluent inspection or its version-matched manual only for a
   configuration/capability uncertainty.
4. Search external literature only where the local evidence is thin and the
   result would affect the experiment design.

Separate reported facts, project observations, inferences, and unknowns. A
useful research brief names the proposed contrast, prerequisite models or
settings, anticipated distinguishing observation, implementation risk, and the
claim it cannot support by itself.

## Challenge the strategy

For an expensive run or a strong inference, use an independent review only when
it can expose a real uncertainty. Give the reviewer the question, parent,
delta, invariants, evidence plan, intended claim, and relevant artifacts—not a
desired answer. Ask it to inspect:

- whether the comparison isolates the proposed cause;
- alternative mechanisms, boundary effects, initialization artifacts, or
  numerical confounders;
- whether the requested histories, fields, and horizon could support the claim;
- prior experiments that make the proposal redundant; and
- the narrowest added observation, control, or sensitivity that would resolve a
  material objection.

Treat review output as evidence to reconcile. Verify deterministic claims in the
records directly and retain only objections that change the experiment or its
claim limit.

## Select a next experiment

Choose the candidate with the strongest expected reduction in the current
uncertainty per unit of compute and implementation risk. Explain the choice by
the specific contrast and evidence it supplies, not by idle hardware or novelty.
When several candidates are plausible, compare them against the same decision
criteria rather than blending their deltas into one uninterpretable case.
