# Scientific lifecycle

The lifecycle protects claims, not process ceremony.

## Discovery

A new or materially changed phase first needs enough contrastive discovery
evidence to identify a specific mechanism/hypothesis worth deeper testing.
Discovery may be short and adaptive.

Before counting a discovery run as evidence, verify its parent/setup, requested
horizon, required outputs, and terminal artifacts.

## Qualification

Before a strong phase claim, run at least one deliberately deep hypothesis
qualification or equivalent linked campaign whose depth matches the claim.
Predeclare the evidence needed to judge it. Do not relabel a short screen as
qualification after seeing a useful result.

## Closure

Conclude when:

- the decisive discovery and qualification evidence exists;
- important competing explanations/assumptions are either tested or reflected
  in the claim limit;
- no feasible in-scope experiment is likely to materially change the phase
  statement.

Otherwise continue, repair, or record a timebox/external block.

An independent subagent may challenge a consequential transition, but
deterministic facts should be checked directly. A reviewer finding is evidence
to resolve, not a request to stop for a human.
