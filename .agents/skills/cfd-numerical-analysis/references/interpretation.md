# Evidence-to-interpretation method

Use this branch after planned evidence exists and a result must be turned into a
bounded scientific statement or durable `results.md` record.

## Read the contract before the result

Start with the experiment question/hypothesis, controlled delta and invariants,
predeclared evidence/figure plan, comparison window, assumptions, and claim
limit. Check every planned core artifact against its intended source, scope,
axes, reduction, and comparison basis. A responsive diagnostic can explain an
unexpected result, but it does not replace a missing planned discriminating
artifact.

If a planned quantity can still be reconstructed from saved data, make it. If it
was never recorded and cannot be reconstructed, classify the exact evidence gap
and its consequence for the claim rather than substituting a convenient proxy.

## Keep the reasoning layers distinct

Write each material statement at one of four levels:

- **Hypothesis** — what was proposed before the evidence;
- **Observation** — what a named run, number, figure, field, or history directly
  records;
- **Interpretation** — a mechanism consistent with those observations and its
  alternatives; and
- **Conclusion** — what this experiment permits under its model and boundaries.

For a consequential claim, name the behaviour, recording artifact, meaningful
comparison, numerical support, alternative explanation, and affected
assumptions. Terms such as stable, converged, better, controlled, or physical
need an explicit metric/window/criterion.

## Judge the intended question

Classify evidence that supports, weakens, or leaves the hypothesis ambiguous;
also state any evidence for the strongest competing explanation. A completed
run is execution evidence, not automatic support. For stationary or bounded
claims, use the declared final window and required continuation/restart evidence
rather than a visually favourable short tail.

External literature can explain or challenge the project observation, but keep
the boundary explicit: project evidence says what this case did; external
knowledge says why it may make sense or what else may explain it.

## Write `results.md`

Put the scientific answer before artifact detail:

1. question, bounded answer, and evidence status;
2. core visual evidence with a caption, direct observation, and limitation;
3. numerical adequacy required to trust that observation;
4. interpretation, alternatives, and claim boundary; and
5. next in-scope action plus compact links to raw histories, manifests, and
   checkpoints.

Embed selected core figures where the reader needs them. Keep raw extracts and
debug plots available but outside the main argument. A no-plot outcome needs a
short explanation of why no valid core visualization exists and the strongest
available evidence matrix instead.
