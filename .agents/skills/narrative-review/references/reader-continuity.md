# Reader continuity

Make the reasoning easy to follow without assuming the reader knows the project.

- Follow each supporting reference. Does the destination actually show the evidence or explanation promised by the sentence? A valid link alone is insufficient.
- Point to the exact figure, panel, curve, equation, or passage. Explain what the reader should notice and how it supports the statement.
- Close small gaps: define unfamiliar terms, explain how to read unusual plots, and distinguish approximate decision values from exact reported values.
- Reuse a clear figure where it makes the connection easier to see. Identify material differences in conditions when using evidence from another run.
- Repair missing support at its source, or narrow the referring statement to what the source supports. Keep unresolved gaps explicit.

Example: “We chose about 200 ms from the coarse-mesh behaviour in §3.6” needs visible support there: a drainage–accumulation plot and an explanation that drainage began to level out while accumulation continued. Point directly to that figure; distinguish the observation-time rationale from the exact 195 ms comparison.

Finish when the reader can find the support, understand it, and follow the inference without supplying a missing step.
