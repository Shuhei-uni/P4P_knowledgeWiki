---
name: narrative-review
description: Review whether readers can trace a written account from purpose through decisions to evidence and interpretation.
---

Test the narrative's causal chain:

- Purpose: Why was this work needed?
- Decision: Why did the chosen approach address that need?
- Setup: Why these parameters, assumptions, and expected outcomes?
- Evidence: What happened, and how did it compare with expectations?
- Interpretation: What follows, what remains uncertain, and what should change?

A durable narrative makes each link explicit and supported. Separate prior expectations from hindsight. Ask for missing reasoning; never invent it.

Explain what the result adds to the research question. State the strongest interpretation the evidence supports, then give its limits.

When presenting facts or results, use a clear figure where it can explain more than prose; see [Sophie Wang's paper-writing guide](https://www.sophielwang.com/blog/paper-writing).
When writing or revising, use the STEM Research Writing references for [grammar](../stem-research-writing/references/grammar-and-sentences.md), [word choice and voice](../stem-research-writing/references/words-and-voice.md), and [flow](../stem-research-writing/references/paragraph-flow.md).

For the final touchup, use [Reader continuity](references/reader-continuity.md) to check supporting references and small explanatory gaps.

Return a brief verdict, the weakest links, and concise repairs.

## Example

After adding film drainage, we tested whether mesh resolution affected predicted collection. We compared the meshes at 195 ms because drainage on the coarse mesh had begun to level out. The finer meshes showed less film coverage, storage, and drainage. We therefore cannot treat lower drainage at this time as evidence of lower eventual collection: the finer meshes may need more time to develop a drainage route. This finding shows why collection must be assessed through film development over time. The available results cannot establish whether drainage will reach similar levels or determine how transport, supply, and release each contributed.
