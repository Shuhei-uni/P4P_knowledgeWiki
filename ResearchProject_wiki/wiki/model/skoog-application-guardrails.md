# Skoog Application Guardrails

## Purpose

This page defines how the project will apply the three-field modelling findings from Skoog (2020) to the geothermal separator without treating the BWR annular-flow case as a directly transferable setup.

This is a project modelling guardrail, not a Fluent setup plan. Provisional branch labels are recorded here so that later setup plans follow one consistent logic.

## Source boundary

Skoog's useful contribution is the modelling architecture:

- separate vapor core, wall film, and dispersed droplets;
- prescribe or calculate an initial film/droplet split;
- distinguish inlet droplets from droplets generated by film entrainment;
- use developed particle profiles or controlled injection velocities to reduce artificial entrance deposition;
- compare film flow, droplet flow, deposition, and entrainment as a coupled mass-transfer system ([skoog-2020], p.7-12, p.16-25, Appendix A/B).

Do not transfer the following directly:

- BWR operating conditions or mass fluxes;
- the Wallis transition result as a geothermal inlet truth;
- Okawa coefficients or critical thresholds without a project sensitivity basis;
- Skoog's `55%` or `60%` transverse-velocity factors as project defaults;
- the Skoog droplet diameter or parcel population as a measured geothermal distribution.

The Skoog source is therefore an implementation and bookkeeping precedent, not validation for geothermal separator values. The reusable source extraction remains in [CFD_wiki's Skoog page](../../../CFD_wiki/wiki/sources/skoog-2020-annular-flow-three-field-cfd-thesis.md).

## Project mass-accounting rule

For the current `1600 kJ/kg` project reference, the observed/reconstructed carrier targets are approximately:

| Quantity | Project reference |
|---|---:|
| Total liquid feed | `116.92 kg/s` |
| Vapor feed | `80.69 kg/s` |
| Total two-phase feed | `197.61 kg/s` |

For any future coupled three-field branch, the liquid representation must satisfy:

```text
total liquid feed
= Eulerian liquid contribution
+ EWF film contribution
+ DPM droplet contribution
```

The same liquid mass must not be imposed once through the Eulerian/Mixture inlet and again through DPM. The Purnanto nine-injection payload is retained as a one-way carryover diagnostic history, not as the default coupled EWF loading.

If the existing carrier field already contains the full `116.92 kg/s` liquid inlet, DPM must remain a one-way diagnostic unless the Eulerian liquid contribution is reduced by the DPM/film contribution. A coupled branch is not physically interpretable until this allocation is explicit.

## Injection-role guardrail

Every injection must have one declared role before it is created:

| Role | Meaning | Skoog-aligned treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Inlet mist | Droplets entering with the steam-side flow | Use a justified fraction of the liquid feed, with controlled velocity/profile; do not use the full Purnanto liquid spectrum automatically. |
| Initial wall film | Liquid already present as a film at the start of the annular/film section | Supply through an EWF wall/inlet condition or an explicitly allocated film contribution. |
| Entrainment droplets | Droplets created from the wall film by interfacial shear | Generate from the local film state through the EWF/entrainment mechanism; do not prescribe them as an independent copy of the liquid feed. |
| Splash/edge/stripping products | Secondary particles created by wall-film interaction | Use Fluent's generated-particle pathway and record the generated mass separately from original inlet DPM mass. |

The project must not use one injection family to represent all four roles.

## Numerical and physical guardrails

1. Establish a closed-enough carrier solution before testing EWF interaction. The current `09c`/`10a` evidence remains debug-only because continuity and whole-domain phase balance are not closed.
2. Start with one mechanism at a time: deposition/absorption, then splash, then edge separation, then stripping.
3. Use the smallest physically justified droplet-size subset first. Add size classes only when they answer a specific sensitivity question.
4. Avoid a uniform inlet particle field when a developed profile or local film-interface injection is the intended physics. Skoog found that inlet-profile choice and transverse velocity strongly affected the artificial entrance-deposition peak ([skoog-2020], p.14-18, p.25-27).
5. Treat transverse velocity as a sensitivity parameter, not a tuned constant. Record the baseline velocity, the tested factor, and the reason for the change.
6. Record field-wise mass histories: vapor, Eulerian liquid if present, EWF film, original DPM, generated DPM, film outflow, and film inventory.
7. Keep global DPM interaction with the continuous phase conceptually separate from EWF-DPM coupling. Enabling EWF coupling does not justify adding an unallocated DPM source to the existing carrier field.
8. For the first EWF stability control, use EWF-DPM coupling with global DPM interaction disabled. Re-enable global DPM source feedback only as a separately named sensitivity after film formation is stable; do not treat a failed coupled run as evidence for splash, edge separation, or stripping.
9. Start the first EWF transient screening run with a conservative, recorded film timestep and monitor film CFL/source spikes after each early step. The referenced server-2 `10a` failure analysis reported a film CFL of approximately `679` at the second film step; the live readback remains `Debug only`.
10. Before running edge separation or stripping, verify that a finite film exists, the correct film walls and edges are active, the film material and injection material properties match, and generated-particle mass can be reported independently.

## Provisional branch sequence

These remain provisional planning labels, but their setup files and lineage are now recorded under `Setups/` and in `Setups/order-dictionary.md`. They should not be treated as accepted or report-quality cases until their gates pass.

### `09cV2` — Skoog partition and injection-formation control

Purpose: establish the film/droplet allocation and injection roles on a stable carrier field.

Required comparison:

- one-way Purnanto diagnostic;
- Skoog-style allocated inlet droplets;
- explicit film contribution or EWF film source;
- no splash, edge separation, or stripping in the first comparison.

Promotion gate: the liquid allocation is explicit and the field-wise mass balance is sufficiently closed to distinguish numerical failure from injection overloading.

### `010V2` — EWF deposition and film-inventory control

Purpose: verify that droplets deposit into the EWF film and that film storage, drainage, and DPM-to-film transfer are bounded.

Required comparison:

- inherit the accepted `09cV2` allocation;
- use global DPM interaction off for the first EWF stability baseline, while retaining EWF-DPM coupling on;
- enable deposition/absorption only;
- report film inventory, film outflow, absorbed DPM mass, and residual/phase-flux behavior.

Promotion gate: absorbed DPM mass, film outflow, film storage, and remaining particle fates can be reconciled within a documented tolerance.

### Later `010V2` derivatives

Only after the `010V2` control is interpretable:

- `010V2a`: splash;
- `010V2b`: edge separation;
- `010V2c`: particle stripping;
- `010V2d`: combined wall-film interaction, only if each isolated mechanism is stable.

The final naming and ordering will be checked against `Setups/order-dictionary.md` when the setup plans are created.

## Claim discipline

Until the carrier and field-wise liquid balance are closed, Skoog-derived branches may support only:

- implementation diagnostics;
- sensitivity trends;
- comparison of injection and film-transport formulations.

They must not yet support a geothermal separator efficiency, steam-purity, or validated re-entrainment claim. Skoog's model can tell us how to structure the experiment; it cannot supply the missing geothermal inlet droplet distribution or wall-film validation data.
